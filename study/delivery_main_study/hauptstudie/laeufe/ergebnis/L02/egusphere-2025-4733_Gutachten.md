## Summary

The manuscript is an observational case study of one brittle fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite of the Bedretto Underground Laboratory (overburden stated as 1 to 1.5 km), characterized as preparation for the FEAR fluid injection experiments. Two boreholes from a common collar at TM2363, BO5 (216 m long, dip 29.5°) and BO6 (55 m, dip 10.3°), cross the fault zone at about 37 to 45 m and 24 to 29 m. The fault zone is subdivided into five core facies (F0 intact rock to F4 cataclasite). Field data comprise optical and acoustic televiewer picks, fracture intensity P10, full wave sonic Vp and Vs with a Gardner density and porosity log, 11 double straddle packer intervals of 0.73 m (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6) evaluated for permeability, static pore pressure and flow dimension, injectivities and two crosshole tests from a second campaign with 2.4 m and 3.7 m intervals. Laboratory data comprise 11 plugs from BO5 (38 mm diameter) measured by pulse decay or steady state at effective isotropic stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa, water immersion and helium porosity, core and plug Vp, XRD and CT. Key values: single-hole interval means from 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ to 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m² in BO5 and 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ to 1.2 × 10⁻¹¹ m² in BO6, crosshole values 1.6 and 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁵ m², plug permeabilities from 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ to 2.0 × 10⁻¹³ m², flow dimensions 1.58 to 1.95. The manuscript concludes that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability because plugs exclude large fractures, that scale dependence also reflects intrinsic network properties, that the fault core is the main flow path without acting as a barrier, and that the fault zone combines localized and distributed conduit behaviour.

## Strengths

1. The plugs and the packer intervals come from the same borehole, and Fig. 2 documents both positions: the interval tops and the plug positions can be measured on the figure, and eight of the eleven plugs lie inside a tested interval (PP-14, PP-41, GB-05). The design therefore holds the material for a pairwise laboratory to field comparison in the same rock, even though the manuscript does not use it.
2. The borehole logs are reported with numbers that can be traced to the figures: Vp mean, extremes, Vs mean, porosity extremes and caliper anomalies in Sect. 4.1 agree with the values measured on Figs 6 and 7, and the field bars of Fig. 10 reproduce the boxplot ranges of Figs 6 and 7 within 0.1 decades (PP-30, PP-38, PP-51). A reader can therefore locate every stated log value in the figures.
3. The laboratory protocol is applied uniformly and is countable: every one of the eleven plugs has values at all three stress stages, every plug name spans 4 cm as the naming rule states, and the counts of samples and intervals agree between text, captions and legends (PP-41, PP-23, PP-22). This lets a reader check the plug inventory against the figures, which is the basis on which several value deviations in this report could be located.
4. Several limits are disclosed at the method they affect, for example the transfer of the stress field from TM1750 to TM2250 to the test volume, the overlap of packers and fractures around the core and the possible packer misplacement at BO5 interval 3, and the reference list is complete in the direction from entry to citation: all 69 entries are cited, with matching years and spellings (PP-19, PP-35, PP-57). The reader can weigh these limits without reconstructing them; where a limit is not repeated at the comparison it affects, this report says so (GB-41).

## Weaknesses

1. The scale effect conclusions go beyond what the design can separate. Plugs and intervals differ in fracture content by selection, the announced co-located comparison is not reported, the laboratory data points in Fig. 9 are repeats with unequal weights, the scale axis of Fig. 10 does not encode measurement dimension, and the conclusion presents an intrinsic network control and a uniform laboratory underestimation that the own data do not show (GB-05, GB-04, GB-56, GB-57, GB-63). Suggestion: report the co-located plug and interval pairs with their ratio, define one data point per specimen and stress stage, redraw Fig. 10 on a measurement dimension axis, and state the mechanism as an interpretation consistent with the literature.
2. The statements on fault zone architecture rest on evidence that does not isolate them: the crosshole tests do not show a path through the core, the damage zone is undefined and described in contradictory terms, the clustering contrast contradicts the Results, the BO6 pore pressures contradict the compartmentalization, and the classification after Caine et al. (1996) does not follow the cited definition (GB-58, GB-59, GB-64, GB-43, GB-06). Suggestion: define the damage zone by facies, report the crosshole monitoring responses and per interval flow dimensions, and restate the architecture as an interpretation where the data do not isolate it.
3. Key evaluations cannot be reproduced: the Gardner coefficients and reference densities, the Thiem inputs and interval lengths of the crosshole tests, the step from test records to boxplots and means, the dates and equipment of the campaigns, the instruments and settings, and the content of the data and code statements are not given (GB-29, GB-34, GB-33, GB-30, GB-32, GB-26, GB-69, GB-68). Suggestion: add a campaign table, an interval table with tests, evaluations and the statistic shown, and a specimen table, and deposit the evaluation code with the data.
4. Several values and identifiers disagree between text, figures and sources: stress magnitudes between text and Fig. 2 and against the cited sources, laboratory permeabilities against Fig. 8, the borehole without Vs, BO6 facies assignments, sample depth labels, four citations without reference entries, and further source attributions (GB-19, GB-18, GB-44, GB-28, GB-41, GB-45, GB-55, GB-54, GB-11). Suggestion: reconcile each value with its figure or source as listed in the findings and complete the reference list.
