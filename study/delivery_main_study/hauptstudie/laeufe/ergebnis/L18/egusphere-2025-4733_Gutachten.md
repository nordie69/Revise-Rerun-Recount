## Summary

The manuscript is an observational multi-scale case study of one fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite, at the Bedretto underground laboratory (overburden 1 to 1.5 km), carried out to prepare the injection experiments of the FEAR project. Two boreholes cross the fault, BO5 (azimuth 31.5°, dip 29.5°) and BO6 (24.7°, 10.3°). The field data are 11 double-straddle packer intervals of 0.73 m (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6) with pulse, constant-rate and shut-in tests evaluated by type curves, Cooper-Jacob, Horner and nSIGHTS, static pore pressures, flow dimensions after the generalized radial flow model, injectivities and two crosshole tests from a second campaign with 2.4 m intervals, plus optical and acoustic televiewer and full-wave sonic logs. The laboratory data are 11 plugs from BO5 tested at effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa by pulse decay or steady state, water immersion and helium porosity, core and plug P-wave velocity, XRD and CT. Results are grouped by five structural facies (F0 to F4). Key numbers: BO5 interval means from 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ to 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m², BO6 from 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ to 1.2 × 10⁻¹¹ m², plugs from 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ to 2.0 × 10⁻¹³ m², crosshole values 1.6 and 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁵ m², flow dimensions 1.58 to 1.95. The manuscript concludes that laboratory tests underestimate in situ permeability because plugs exclude large fractures, that the scale effect also reflects fracture connectivity, that the main flow path runs through the fault core, and that the fault zone combines localized and distributed conduit behaviour without a continuous low-permeability core.

## Strengths

1. The design brings plugs at three effective stresses, 11 packer intervals in two boreholes, two crosshole tests and published Bedretto data into one comparison; plug and interval counts agree between text and figures (PP-33, PP-46), and the laboratory to field offset of several orders of magnitude for facies 1 to 3 does not depend on unequal case numbers (PP-43).
2. Most limitations are disclosed where the method is introduced: overlapping packers around the core, dry and unconfined laboratory Vp, the empirical porosity log, the transfer of the stress field from TM2000 and the second campaign with other equipment (PP-17, PP-35); a reader can therefore see which values are model-derived, even where the consequences are not carried through (GB-17, GB-20).
3. Where the figures were measured, most values in the text agree with them: interval positions and lengths in Fig. 2, the stated means of most intervals, the BO5 pore pressures, Vp ranges and most laboratory extremes (PP-19, PP-37, PP-38, PP-40, PP-42); the deviations found concern single values (GB-25, GB-35).
4. Values taken from the geological and stress literature are reproduced correctly (PP-05, PP-07, PP-10, PP-15), every one of the 69 reference entries is cited in the text, and the data are deposited openly under CC BY 4.0 (PP-55, PP-57).

## Weaknesses

1. The scale-effect statements go beyond the data: the attribution to fracture connectivity has no own test and overstates the cited source (GB-47), "consistently" low laboratory values and the general underestimation ignore a facies 4 plug that exceeds its co-located interval (GB-58), Fig. 9 weights facies by a factor of seven and pools stress stages (GB-49), and the crosshole values are plotted at a scale they do not have (GB-33). Restrict abstract and conclusion to the two measured support scales, add the announced paired comparison of plugs and intervals, and redraw Fig. 9 with one value per plug and stage.
2. The fault-architecture conclusions rest on readings the data do not carry: two crosshole tests with mostly non-core intervals for the core as main flow path (GB-52), a BO6 core interval with lower pore pressure than its neighbours for the pressurized core (GB-32), an F0 section below the BO6 core that does not meet the F0 definition (GB-34), an undefined damage zone for the Caine classification (GB-53), and facies statements contradicted by Figs. 2 and 7 (GB-29, GB-30). Delimit the damage zone, give facies, pressure and permeability per interval, and phrase the flow-path and compartmentalization statements as hypotheses unless the data are extended.
3. The field and laboratory evaluations cannot be rebuilt: no per-interval results, no defined mean, no flow dimension in the Results, no protocol per plug, an unreproducible porosity log, unmarked extrapolated pore pressures, an unidentified second campaign, code only on request and a data statement without content list (GB-23, GB-25, GB-26, GB-12, GB-17, GB-19, GB-20, GB-63, GB-64). Add an interval table and a sample table, state the porosity-log parameters, date the campaigns and deposit the evaluation code with the data.
4. Several text and figure values disagree and the outlook is not tied to the results: stress magnitudes (GB-08), the borehole without Vs (GB-16), laboratory extremes against Fig. 8 (GB-35), four citations without reference entry (GB-48), the depth labels of Fig. 11 (GB-54), the unit of Fig. 5 (e) (GB-21), and recommendations and the stated purpose without a result behind them (GB-59, GB-60, GB-62). Harmonize the values and restrict the outlook to what the measurements show.
