## Summary

The manuscript is an observational field and laboratory characterisation of one crystalline fault zone, the MC fault at about TM2400 of the Bedretto Underground Laboratory, as preparation for the FEAR fault-activation experiments. Two inclined boreholes (BO5, BO6) were logged with optical and acoustic televiewer, caliper and full-wave sonic tools and tested in 11 double-packer intervals of 0.73 m (pulse, constant-rate and shut-in tests evaluated with nSIGHTS and type curves; GRF flow dimension); two crosshole values and injectivities come from a second campaign with 2.4 m intervals (one of 3.7 m). Eleven plugs from facies F1 to F4 were tested in a flow cell by pulse decay and steady state at 15, 25 and 32 MPa effective isotropic stress, together with WIP and He-pycnometry porosity, core and plug Vp, XRD and CT. Field interval means range from 5.6e-16 to 1.6e-11 m² (BO5) and 3.4e-14 to 1.2e-11 m² (BO6), plug permeabilities from about 2e-19 to 3e-13 m², crosshole values 1.6e-15 and 3.9e-15 m², flow dimensions about 1.6 to 2.0. The authors conclude that laboratory tests underestimate in situ permeability because plugs exclude large open fractures, that the fault core is the main flow path, and that the zone combines localized and distributed conduit behaviour.

## Strengths

1. The same two boreholes were characterised with televiewer picks, fracture intensity, packer tests, pore pressures and plugs from tested positions, so field and laboratory permeability can be grouped by facies and compared for one fault zone (PP-04, PP-15, PP-16).
2. Pulse decay and steady-state techniques together cover plug permeabilities from about 2e-19 to 3e-13 m² at three effective stress levels, so the stress dependence of each plug is visible in Fig. 8 (PP-18).
3. The compilation of Bedretto permeability data in Fig. 10 places the 11 plug and 11 interval ranges of this study next to earlier site data, which makes the difference between laboratory and borehole scale visible (PP-24, PP-25).
4. The geological setting reproduces its main source accurately, and limits such as overlapping packers near the core, the possibly misplaced packer of interval 3 and the thin facies of BO6 are disclosed, which lets a reader weigh individual intervals (PP-06, PP-07, PP-04).

## Weaknesses

1. The hydraulic architecture is stated more firmly than the data allow: two crosshole values from another campaign cannot single out the core as main flow path, the "pressurized core" does not hold in BO6, and the link between clustering and flow regime rests on one interval. A paired crosshole design (core and off-core injection with equal intervals, monitored in both boreholes) or a weaker wording is needed (GB-32, GB-35, GB-33).
2. The scale comparison is confounded and only pooled: laboratory stresses lie at or above the in situ effective stresses, the announced pairwise comparison of plugs with their intervals is missing, Fig. 9 counts repeated measurements and assigns intervals to facies without a rule, and connectivity is claimed but not measured. Plug values at in situ effective stress compared pairwise with their intervals would test the scale effect directly (GB-11, GB-09, GB-31, GB-36).
3. Several evaluation chains cannot be rebuilt: test evaluation parameters, the porosity log from Vp, the second campaign and the Thiem inputs, the interval depths, the averaging behind "mean" values and the data and code deposit are not specified. A methods table per test type, an interval table and a source-by-source availability statement would close this (GB-13, GB-15, GB-17, GB-20, GB-21, GB-38).
4. Numbers in the text disagree with the figures in several places: stress magnitudes (text against Fig. 2), BO6 facies, pore pressure and injectivity statements against Fig. 7, the BO6 interval-1 mean against its boxplot, plug values against Fig. 8, and four citations without reference entry. Each value should be checked against its figure (GB-04, GB-24, GB-21, GB-26, GB-30).
