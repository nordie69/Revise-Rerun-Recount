## Summary

The manuscript reports a field and laboratory characterization of the MC fault zone in the Rotondo granite of the BedrettoLab, carried out to prepare fluid injection experiments of the FEAR project. Two boreholes, BO5 (216 m, dip 29.5°) and BO6 (55 m, dip 10.3°), cross the fault at about 37 to 45 m and 24 to 29 m. Eleven intervals of 0.73 m were tested with a double-straddle packer (pulse, constant rate and shut-in tests, evaluated with nSIGHTS, Bredehoeft and Papadopulos, Cooper and Jacob, Horner and the generalized radial flow model), two crosshole tests were evaluated with Thiem's equation, and injectivities come from a second campaign with 2.4 m intervals; televiewer and full wave sonic logs give fracture intensity, velocities and a Vp-derived porosity. Eleven plugs from BO5, grouped into four fault facies, were measured at effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa by pulse decay or steady state, together with immersion porosimetry, He-pycnometry, P-wave velocity, XRD and CT. Field permeabilities range from 5.6 × 10^-16 to 1.6 × 10^-11 m² (interval means in BO5) and 3.4 × 10^-14 to 1.2 × 10^-11 m² (BO6), laboratory values from about 10^-19 to 3 × 10^-13 m², crosshole values are 1.6 and 3.9 × 10^-15 m², flow dimensions 1.58 to 1.95. The authors conclude that permeability is scale dependent, that the fault core is the main flow path with a weakly developed damage zone, and that the fault is a mixed localized and distributed conduit without a continuous low-permeability core.

## Strengths

1. Plugs were taken where the packer intervals were set: 8 of 11 plugs lie inside a tested interval, and each plug was measured at three effective stresses (PP-07, PP-15). This makes a paired comparison of laboratory and field permeability at the same location possible, which the manuscript prepares but does not yet carry out (GB-02).
2. The field results are reported per interval as boxplots with outliers, with pore pressure, fracture intensity and injectivity on the same depth axis (Figs. 6, 7). Measured against the figures, the stated means and pore pressures agree for most intervals (PP-17, PP-18), so a reader can check the field values interval by interval.
3. The geological and stress context is reproduced faithfully from the cited works: stress magnitudes and orientation, facies definitions, tunnel positions and granite varieties match the sources that could be opened (PP-01), which makes the setting of the test volume traceable.
4. Limits are stated where they arise in the results: possible packer misplacement at interval 3, facies in BO6 too thin to isolate, the size limit of plugs, and the schematic nature of Fig. 12 (PP-04, PP-09). The reader can see which interpretations rest on assumptions.

## Weaknesses

1. The conclusion that the main flow path is concentrated in the fault core rests on two crosshole tests, one injecting outside the core and neither isolating core from damage zone, with low permeabilities the manuscript itself places at the lower end of the field values (GB-29). The pressurized core with isolated flanks is seen in BO5 but not in BO6 (GB-32). Add a crosshole test with separately packed-off core and damage zone intervals in the observation borehole, or restrict the conclusion to what the two tests show.
2. The scale comparison pools laboratory stress stages up to 32 MPa, above every in-situ principal stress, weights facies differently through undefined data point counts, lacks the host rock facies at both scales, and is not done pairwise although the sampling allows it (GB-02, GB-10, GB-26). Compare each plug with the interval it was taken from at that interval's in-situ effective stress, and show Fig. 9 with one value per plug and interval.
3. Several values in the text disagree with the figures or with other parts of the text: vertical stress, Vs logging, facies of BO6 intervals, the mean of BO6 interval 1, the laboratory ranges of facies three and four, the porosity comparison in Fig. A1 and the depths in Fig. 11 (GB-07, GB-13, GB-18, GB-19, GB-20, GB-22, GB-23, GB-31). Check all values against the data and add tables per plug and per interval (GB-12).
4. Methods and documentation do not allow the results to be rebuilt: the stated aim of deriving site-selection constraints is not carried through, the facies rule for F0 is not met by the F0 sections of BO6, the Vp-to-porosity relation and the test evaluation parameters are incomplete, the second campaign is undated, four cited sources lack entries, and the data statement does not say which data are deposited (GB-01, GB-08, GB-11, GB-14, GB-27, GB-34). Complete Sects. 3 and 6, the reference list and the data statement accordingly.
