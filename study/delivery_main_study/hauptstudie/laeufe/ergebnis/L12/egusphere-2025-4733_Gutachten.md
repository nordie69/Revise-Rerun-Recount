## Summary

The manuscript is a field and laboratory characterisation of one fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite at the Bedretto Underground Laboratory, carried out to prepare the FEAR fluid-injection experiments. Two inclined boreholes from the same mouth, BO5 (216 m, dip 29.5 degrees) and BO6 (55 m, dip 10.3 degrees), cross the fault at about 37 to 45 m and 24 to 29 m. The fault zone is divided into five structural facies (F0 to F4). In the field the authors use optical and acoustic televiewer, caliper and full-wave sonic logs, eleven double-straddle packer intervals of 0.73 m (seven in BO5, four in BO6) evaluated with nSIGHTS, type curves, Cooper-Jacob and Horner methods, and two crosshole tests from a second campaign. In the laboratory, eleven plugs from BO5 were tested for permeability with pulse decay or steady state at isotropic effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa, alongside water-immersion and helium porosity, P-wave velocity, XRD and CT. Mean interval permeabilities range from 5.6e-16 to 1.6e-11 m2 in BO5 and from 3.4e-14 to 1.2e-11 m2 in BO6; lab values range from 2.0e-19 to 2.0e-13 m2; the crosshole tests give 1.6e-15 and 3.9e-15 m2, and flow dimensions are 1.58 to 1.95. The authors conclude that permeability depends on scale, that the fault core is the main flow path, and that the zone combines localized and distributed conduit behaviour without a continuous low-permeability core.

## Strengths

1. All field logs, packer intervals and plug positions come from the same two boreholes and are shown on one depth axis per borehole. This makes a co-located lab-to-field comparison possible; interval and sample counts and positions agree between text and Fig. 2 (PP-06, PP-11).
2. Every plug was tested at three effective stresses with a technique matched to its permeability, so the stress sensitivity of each facies is visible. The ranges stated for facies one, two and four agree with the measured values in Fig. 8 (PP-13, PP-15).
3. Field permeability is shown as a distribution per interval, together with static pore pressure, injectivity and fracture intensity. For BO5 the values given in the text agree with the boxplots and markers measured in Fig. 6 (PP-12, PP-14).
4. The results are placed in the Bedretto context: Fig. 10 compiles site permeabilities by scale, and the geological and stress settings render the cited sources correctly. Flow dimensions in the text match Fig. 11 (PP-01, PP-17).

## Weaknesses

1. The interpretation of flow architecture goes beyond the data. Two crosshole tests do not single out the fault core as the main flow path, and the pressurised core holds in BO5 but not in BO6. The practical recommendations, independence of the opposite tunnel side and intervals free of neighbouring fractures, are not derived from results, and the announced selection of injection sites is not reported. Suggestion: restrict the claims to what the tests show and add a section that links results to selection criteria (GB-30, GB-32, GB-35, GB-36, GB-01).
2. The lab-to-field scale comparison is confounded and partly mis-plotted. Lab stresses partly exceed in-situ effective stresses and all stages are pooled; N in Fig. 9 counts repeated points; crosshole values sit at 25 to 300 m though the boreholes are 8 to 13 m apart; the promised co-located comparison is missing; and the Conclusion generalises "consistently low" lab values. Suggestion: compare paired samples at matched stress and plot each value at its true scale (GB-09, GB-27, GB-29, GB-07, GB-34).
3. Reproducibility is limited. The porosity log is a Vp transform with unstated constants, which gives negative porosities. The hydraulic evaluation chain is not specified, a second test campaign is undocumented, per-sample measurements are not tabulated, and the data statement does not say what the repository holds. Suggestion: add method details, a test table, a sample table and a described data and code deposit (GB-11, GB-12, GB-13, GB-24, GB-37).
4. Several statements disagree with the figures or are not recoverable from them: the averaging of interval values and the value for BO6 interval 1, the facies of BO6 interval 2, the BO6 core pore pressure, the BO6 to BO5 fracture-intensity ratio, the facies three lab range, the facies definitions against the fracture picks, and four missing reference entries. Suggestion: correct the text against the figures and add the missing entries (GB-17, GB-19, GB-20, GB-21, GB-23, GB-06, GB-28).
