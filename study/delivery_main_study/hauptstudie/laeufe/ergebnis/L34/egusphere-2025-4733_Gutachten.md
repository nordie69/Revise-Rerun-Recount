## Summary

The manuscript is an observational field and laboratory characterisation of a single fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite at the Bedretto Underground Laboratory, as the target of the FEAR fault activation experiments. Two boreholes cross the fault (BO5, 216 m, dip 29.5°; BO6, 55 m, dip 10.3°). Methods are optical and acoustic televiewer logging with a five-class facies scheme (F0 to F4), full wave sonic logging with a porosity log derived from Vp through the Gardner relation, double-packer tests in 11 intervals of 0.73 m (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6) evaluated with nSIGHTS, type curves, Cooper-Jacob, Horner and the generalized radial flow model, injectivity and two crosshole tests from a second campaign, and laboratory tests on 11 plugs from BO5 (pulse decay and steady state at effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa) with water immersion porosimetry, He-pycnometry, MSCL and acoustic emission velocities, XRD and CT. Reported key values are interval mean permeabilities of 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ to 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m² (BO5) and 3.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ to 1.2 × 10⁻¹¹ m² (BO6), laboratory permeabilities of 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ to 2.0 × 10⁻¹³ m², crosshole permeabilities of 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ and 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁵ m² and flow dimensions of 1.58 to 1.95. The authors conclude that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability (scale effect), that the main flow path runs through the fault core, that the fault core is pressurised and bounded by isolated flanks, and that the zone is a mix of localized and distributed conduit.

## Strengths

1. The sampling was designed so that plugs come from the tested packer intervals; measured on Fig. 2, 8 of the 11 plugs lie inside an interval, so the data for a pairwise comparison of laboratory and field permeability at the same depth exist (GB-14, PP-15).
2. Facies boundaries, interval bars and sampling positions are drawn consistently across Fig. 2, Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 (boundaries agree within 0.02 to 0.03 m, sampling lines within 0.02 m of the plug names), which lets a reader locate every interval and plug in the facies log (PP-13, PP-14, PP-16).
3. Most values stated in the Results can be reproduced from the figures by measurement, among them Vp and Vs means and extremes, porosity extremes, static pore pressures, the injectivity maximum, the crosshole values and the range of flow dimensions (PP-26, PP-27, PP-37, PP-38).
4. The regional setting and the stress field reproduce their cited sources closely (overburden, granite age and extent, stress magnitudes and orientation), and every one of the 69 reference entries is cited in the text (PP-06, PP-07, PP-10, PP-47).

## Weaknesses

1. Two statements on the hydraulic architecture of the fault, that the main flow path runs through the fault core and that the core is pressurised and bounded by isolated flanks, are not carried by the data: the two crosshole tests cannot locate a flow path and the BO6 pore pressures contradict the pattern. They should be stated as hypotheses, with the crosshole inputs reported and a decisive test named (GB-36, GB-49, GB-50, GB-52).
2. The scale effect, the main result, is shown only as pooled distributions: the co-located plug and interval pairs are not evaluated, Fig. 9 weights repeated readings, the facies assignment of mixed intervals is not stated, facies 0 is missing and the highest laboratory stress exceeds the in-situ stresses. A table pairing each plug with its interval and a Fig. 9 with one value per plug and interval would answer these points (GB-13, GB-14, GB-18, GB-34, GB-48, GB-53).
3. The field evaluation cannot be rebuilt: which values enter each interval mean and which mean is reported, values for three BO5 intervals, flow dimensions per interval, the porosity formula, the marking of extrapolated pore pressures, devices, campaign dates and the code are not given. A per-interval table in Sect. 3.2.2 or 4.1 and deposited code would close this gap (GB-16, GB-17, GB-20, GB-22, GB-23, GB-24, GB-25, GB-27, GB-28, GB-29).
4. Several moderate inconsistencies between text, figures and reference list remain, among them the stress values of Fig. 2, the borehole without Vs, laboratory extremes against Fig. 8, two figures with different porosities for one core segment, the strike of the MC fault, four citations without reference entry, the site selection aim and two outlook recommendations. Each should be reconciled at the place named in the finding (GB-01, GB-11, GB-15, GB-19, GB-37, GB-43, GB-47, GB-56).
