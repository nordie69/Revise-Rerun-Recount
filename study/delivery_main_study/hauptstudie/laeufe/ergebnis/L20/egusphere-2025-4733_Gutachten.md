## Summary

The manuscript is an observational case study of one fault zone, the MC fault in the Bedretto Underground Laboratory (Rotondo granite, overburden about 1 to 1.5 km), prepared as site characterisation for the FEAR fault activation experiments. Two inclined boreholes, BO5 (216 m, dip 29.5°) and BO6 (55 m, dip 10.3°), were logged with optical and acoustic televiewer and full wave sonic, and tested hydraulically with a double straddle packer in eleven 0.73 m intervals (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6) by pulse, constant rate and shut-in tests evaluated with type curves, Cooper and Jacob, Horner and nSIGHTS; injectivities and two crosshole permeabilities come from a second campaign with a 2.4 m interval. Eleven plugs from BO5, grouped into the structural facies F1 to F4 of a five-facies scheme (F0 to F4), were tested by pulse decay or steady state at isotropic effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa; porosity was measured by water immersion porosimetry and He-pycnometry and estimated along the boreholes from Vp with Gardner's relation. Key numbers: field interval means from 5.6 × 10^-16 to 1.6 × 10^-11 m² (BO5) and 3.4 × 10^-14 to 1.2 × 10^-11 m² (BO6), laboratory values from 2.0 × 10^-19 to 2.0 × 10^-13 m², crosshole values 1.6 × 10^-15 and 3.9 × 10^-15 m², flow dimensions between 1.58 and 1.95. The authors conclude that permeability is controlled by open and connected fractures, that laboratory plugs underestimate in-situ permeability because they exclude large fractures, and that the fault zone is a mix of localized and distributed conduit after Caine et al. (1996) without a continuous low-permeability core.

## Strengths

1. The sampling design ties the two scales together: plugs were taken in the same borehole and, where feasible, at the positions of the hydraulic intervals, and every plug was tested at the same three effective stresses. The counts are internally consistent (7 plus 4 intervals, 11 plugs at 3 stages, 33 values in Fig. 8; PP-15), and 8 of the 11 plugs lie inside a tested interval (GB-04), so a pairwise lab to field comparison is possible from the authors' own data.
2. Limits are disclosed at many places: the transfer of the stress field from TM2000 to the test volume, the restriction of samples to BO5, overlapping packers around the core, the second campaign with other equipment and the caution about facies hydraulics in BO6 are stated in the text (PP-14). This lets a reader see where the comparison is weakest, although several of these limits are not carried through (GB-25, GB-35).
3. The figure set is coherent in its numbering and in most of its numbers: figures 1 to 12 and A1 to A4 are numbered without gaps, the main figures are cited in order, and the measured interval positions, pore pressures, box ranges and laboratory extremes in Figs. 2, 6, 7, A1 and A2 agree with the text within reading accuracy (PP-09, PP-11, PP-24, PP-25, PP-29, PP-33).
4. The introductory statements that were checked against their sources hold: nucleation depth and loss of information by scattering (Achtziger-Zupančič et al., 2024), the BedrettoLab overburden (Ma et al., 2022) and the prediction half of the forecasting sentence are covered by the cited works (PP-05, PP-06, PP-07), and Fig. 10 places the authors' data in a compilation of Bedretto data from eight sources (PP-40).

## Weaknesses

1. The causal reading of the scale effect is stronger than the design separates. Laboratory plugs differ from the field intervals not only in scale but in selection (they exclude open fractures by construction) and in stress state, the comparison is reported only facies-wise, and the conclusion describes a monotonic growth of spread and connectivity control with scale that the manuscript's own Fig. 10 and crosshole values do not show (GB-04, GB-55, GB-54). Suggestion: add the pairwise table of the 8 plug and interval pairs at a stated effective stress, and restrict abstract and conclusion to what this comparison shows.
2. The hydraulic architecture rests on few and partly indirect observations: the control by open and connected fractures is a visual co-location without a measured relation, the core as main flow path is attributed to two crosshole tests that do not isolate it and gave values three to four orders below the single-hole core values, the compartmentalization pattern appears in one of two boreholes, and the flow dimensions behind the clustering conclusion are not reported (GB-03, GB-49, GB-28, GB-56). Suggestion: report an interval table with open fracture counts, flow dimensions and pore pressures, and phrase the architecture as consistent with the data unless a crosshole design with a comparison path is added.
3. The field and laboratory evaluation cannot be rebuilt from the manuscript: there is no table of the eleven intervals, the unit of a "data point" in Fig. 9 and the facies assignment of mixed intervals are not defined, the route from tests to the plotted value, the Horner extrapolations and the second campaign are not documented, and data and code availability are only partly specified (GB-29, GB-46, GB-35, GB-23, GB-24, GB-25, GB-60, GB-61). Suggestion: add an interval table and a plug table in Sects. 3 and 4 and map the Zenodo record to these data sources.
4. Several moderate inconsistencies between text, figures and references need correction: the borehole without Vs, the stress magnitudes of Fig. 2, three laboratory values against Fig. 8, the Gardner-based porosity log with unstated reference densities, the missing facies 0 in the laboratory set, four cited works without reference entries, and the strike of the MC fault in the plan views (GB-21, GB-12, GB-38, GB-22, GB-17, GB-45, GB-19). Suggestion: correct each at the stated location and state the sources of the values in the captions.
