## Summary

The manuscript is an observational field and laboratory characterisation of one fault zone (the MC fault) in the Rotondo granite of the Bedretto Underground Laboratory at about 1 km depth, carried out to prepare the FEAR stimulation experiments. Two boreholes from TM2363 (BO5, 216 m; BO6, 55 m) cross the fault zone, which is divided into five structural facies (F0 to F4). The authors combine optical and acoustic televiewer logs, full wave sonic logs with a Vp-derived porosity log, eleven double-straddle packer intervals of 0.73 m (seven in BO5, four in BO6) evaluated with type curves, Cooper-Jacob, Horner and the generalized radial flow model, two crosshole tests from another campaign with 2.4 m and 3.7 m intervals, and laboratory measurements on eleven plugs from BO5 at effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa (pulse decay and steady state), with WIP, He-pycnometry, P-wave velocity, XRD and CT in the appendix. Reported field permeabilities range from 5.6 × 10^-16 to 1.6 × 10^-11 m² as interval means in BO5 and up to 1.2 × 10^-11 m² in BO6; laboratory values range from 2.0 × 10^-19 to 2.0 × 10^-13 m²; the crosshole tests yield 1.6 × 10^-15 and 3.9 × 10^-15 m². The authors conclude that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability, that the fault core is the main flow path without a continuous low-permeability barrier, and that the fault is a mix of localized and distributed conduit with flow dimensions between 1.58 and 1.95.

## Strengths

1. The study measures the same fault zone at two scales with consistent sample bookkeeping: eleven intervals (7 + 4) and eleven plugs (3 + 2 + 4 + 2) agree between methods, text and figures (PP-23, PP-26, PP-27), so the facies-wise comparison of laboratory and field permeability rests on a stated and countable sample (PP-28).
2. Most log values in the text are reproducible from the figures: measured Vp, Vs, porosity extremes, pore pressures, injectivities and BO5 box positions agree with Sect. 4.1 within the measurement uncertainty (PP-29, PP-30, PP-35), and the test interval bars of Fig. 2 agree with the box depths of Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 within 0.05 m (PP-13).
3. The manuscript discloses several of its own limits where they arise: plugs cannot contain large apertures, BO6 intervals overlap several facies, and the packer placement in BO5 interval 3 may have been inaccurate (PP-34, PP-39, PP-20); this lets a reader weigh the interval results, though not all of these limits are carried into Fig. 9 and the conclusion (GB-29).
4. The geological setting uses its sources accurately: stress magnitudes, massif dimensions, intrusion age and facies descriptions match Bröker and Ma (2022), Rast et al. (2022) and Achtziger-Zupančič et al. (2024) (PP-03, PP-06, PP-07), and every entry of the reference list is cited (PP-49).

## Weaknesses

1. The statement that the main flow passes through the fault core rests on two crosshole tests, one of which injects outside the core, and the pressure pattern behind the compartmentalisation argument is not consistent in BO6 and has no hydrostatic reference (GB-44, GB-30, GB-46). Report all crosshole tests with geometry and monitoring response, state which BO6 interval is the core and the hydrostatic reference, and phrase the flow path statement as a hypothesis until a crosshole design with core and non-core pairs is available.
2. The scale effect is shown on pooled facies distributions only: co-located plug and interval pairs are announced but not reported, the laboratory N in Fig. 9 are seven times the measurements for three facies, the laboratory stages include stresses above in-situ values, and the conclusion attributes the gap to intrinsic network control without own data (GB-48, GB-41, GB-14, GB-47). Report the plug and interval pairs, plot one value per plug and stage at in-situ effective stress, and restrict the causal statement to what the data show.
3. The facies assignment and the per-interval field results cannot be reproduced: BO6 interval 2 is assigned to facies three although it contains none, the facies definitions conflict with the P10 logs, the BO5 interval values are incomplete, and the flow dimensions and test evaluation are not reported per interval (GB-29, GB-12, GB-23, GB-24, GB-18). Add a table of all eleven intervals with depth, facies shares, test types, evaluation, permeability, pore pressure, injectivity and flow dimension, and state the rule that assigns an interval to a facies.
4. Several methods, data and values cannot be checked: the Vp-derived porosity does not follow from Gardner's relation with any single grain density, the second campaign is undated, code is available only on request, the data statement does not say what the record contains, and text and figures disagree on stress values, laboratory extremes, a borehole for Vs and four missing references (GB-16, GB-20, GB-53, GB-54, GB-10, GB-34, GB-15, GB-42). State the porosity equation and densities, add a campaign table, deposit the code, list the record contents, and make text, figures and reference list agree.
