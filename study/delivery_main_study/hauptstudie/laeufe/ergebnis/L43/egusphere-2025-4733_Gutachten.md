## Summary

The manuscript reports an observational single-site characterisation of the MC fault zone in the Bedretto Underground Laboratory (Rotondo granite, overburden 1 to 1.5 km), carried out to prepare the injection experiments of the FEAR project. Two boreholes cross the fault zone, BO5 (216 m, plunge 29.5°) and BO6 (55 m, plunge 10.3°). Field data comprise double-straddle-packer pulse, constant-rate and shut-in tests in 11 intervals of 0.73 m (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6), evaluated with nSIGHTS, type curves, Cooper-Jacob, Horner and the generalized radial flow model; two crosshole tests and step-rate injectivities from a second, unnamed campaign; optical and acoustic televiewer logs with the linear fracture intensity P10; and full wave sonic logs, from which a porosity log is derived with a Gardner-type relation. Eleven plugs from BO5, covering facies F1 to F4 of a five-facies scheme, were tested for permeability at effective isotropic stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa, complemented by water immersion and helium porosity, core-logger and ultrasonic P-wave velocity, XRD and CT. Field interval means range from 5.6 × 10⁻¹⁶ to 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ m² (4.46 orders of magnitude), laboratory values from 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁹ to 2.0 × 10⁻¹³ m², and the two crosshole values are 1.6 and 3.9 × 10⁻¹⁵ m². The authors conclude that open fractures control permeability, that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability, that the fault core is the main and pressurized flow path, and that the zone combines localized and distributed conduit behaviour. The consolidation yields 62 substantive findings (4 major, 35 moderate, 23 minor), 46 minor corrections and 46 verification log entries.

## Strengths

1. The design combines single-hole straddle-packer tests in 11 intervals of 0.73 m with laboratory plugs taken, where feasible, from the tested intervals; 8 of the 11 plugs lie inside five BO5 intervals (PP-17, PP-19), so the data set already holds co-located laboratory and field pairs on which the scale question can be tested directly (GB-13).
2. Values printed in the text agree with the plotted data for most measured quantities: Vp, Vs, porosity and pore pressure means and extremes in Figs. 6 and 7 within about 1 %, the field bars of Fig. 10 within 0.07 decades of the boxplots, and the flow dimension range of Fig. 11 (PP-28, PP-32, PP-41, PP-43), so a reader can trace these numbers to the figures.
3. The manuscript discloses nine limits of its own data, among them overlapping packers near the core, the facies overlap of the BO6 intervals, the second test campaign and the limited separability of sampling bias and intrinsic scale effects (PP-15), and it reports the possibly misplaced packer of BO5 interval 3 instead of omitting the interval (PP-29); this lets a reader locate where results are uncertain, although several consequences are not drawn (GB-23, GB-34).
4. The geological, depth and stress framework of the test volume is reproduced correctly from its sources (Rast et al., 2022; Ceccato et al., 2024; Bröker and Ma, 2022; Achtziger-Zupančič et al., 2024; Ma et al., 2022), so the setting can be relied on (PP-08, PP-09, PP-10, PP-16), and the field and laboratory data are deposited openly under a DOI (PP-45).

## Weaknesses

1. The scale effect, the first conclusion and the title topic, is inferred from facies-pooled distributions in which repeated readings of 2 to 4 plugs count as independent points and field intervals spanning several facies are assigned by an unstated rule, while the co-located pairs are not reported and abstract and conclusion omit the facies 4 exception and the decrease at the crosshole scale (GB-03, GB-13, GB-47, GB-51); report the pairs, use one value per specimen and stress stage, state the facies rule and restrict the claims to what the pairs show.
2. The conclusions on the hydraulic architecture go beyond the data: the main flow path through the core rests on two crosshole tests whose values lie 3.5 to 4.0 decades below the core values and one of which did not inject into the core, the pressurized core is not seen in BO6, and the link of flow regimes to clustering rests on one interval (GB-22, GB-53, GB-56, GB-58); base these statements on the single-hole profiles, report the crosshole pressure responses, and restrict the compartmentalization to BO5 or explain BO6.
3. The field results cannot be rebuilt from the manuscript: the second campaign is neither identified nor dated, the evaluation code is available only on request, interval values, test counts and the type of mean are not tabulated, the crosshole parameters are missing, and the Gardner porosity log uses undocumented constants and grain density (GB-19, GB-21, GB-23, GB-29, GB-32, GB-37, GB-61); add an interval table, a campaign timeline, the code and the complete Gardner parameters with units.
4. Several statements in text and figures disagree, among them the borehole without Vs, four laboratory extremes against Fig. 8, the BO6 facies attribution, the stress magnitudes in Fig. 2, the definition of core Vp and four missing references among the sources of Fig. 10 (GB-09, GB-18, GB-35, GB-38, GB-42, GB-50); a systematic cross-check of each number and label in the text against the figures and the reference list would remove them.
