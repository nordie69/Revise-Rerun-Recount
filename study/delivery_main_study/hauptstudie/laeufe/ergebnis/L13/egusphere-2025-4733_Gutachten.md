## Summary

The manuscript is an observational, multi-scale hydraulic and petrophysical characterisation of one natural fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite of the Bedretto Underground Laboratory (TM2363, overburden given as 1 to 1.5 km). Two inclined boreholes cross the fault zone, BO5 (216 m, analysed 34 to 48 m) and BO6 (55 m, analysed 24 to 34 m). Field data comprise optical and acoustic televiewer logs with linear fracture intensity P10, full wave sonic Vp (and Vs in one borehole), a porosity log derived from Vp with Gardner's relation, 11 straddle packer intervals of 0.73 m (7 in BO5, 4 in BO6) tested by pulse, constant rate and shut-in phases and evaluated with nSIGHTS, type curves, Cooper-Jacob and Horner methods, Generalized Radial Flow dimensions, static pore pressures, and injectivities and two crosshole tests from a second campaign with 2.4 m intervals. Laboratory data comprise 11 plugs from BO5 tested at isotropic effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa by pulse decay and steady state flow, water immersion porosimetry on 12 core segments and their plugs, He-pycnometry on four plugs, Vp on cores and plugs, XRD and CT. Reported interval mean permeabilities range from 5.6 × 10-16 to 1.6 × 10-11 m², laboratory values from 2.0 × 10-19 to 2.0 × 10-13 m², crosshole values are 1.6 × 10-15 and 3.9 × 10-15 m², and flow dimensions lie between 1.58 and 1.95. The authors conclude that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability because large fractures are excluded, that the fault core is the main flow path without a continuous low-permeability core, and that the core is pressurised and hydraulically compartmentalised.

## Strengths

1. Laboratory plugs were taken from the same borehole and at the depths of the packer intervals, so lab and field values from one fault zone at about 1 km overburden can be set against each other; plug and interval counts agree across text, Fig. 2 and Fig. 8 (11 and 11, PP-19, PP-22), and 8 of the 11 plugs lie inside 5 of the 7 BO5 intervals, which makes a paired comparison possible (GB-17).
2. Each interval was tested with several test phases and evaluation methods, and the interval statistics in the text can be traced to the plotted boxplots, pore pressures and injectivities for 10 of the 11 intervals (PP-31, PP-36, PP-38), so most field values stated in Sect. 4.1 are checkable against the figures.
3. The geological and stress context is taken over accurately from the cited sources: the stress magnitudes and orientation from Bröker and Ma (2022), the tunnel geology from Rast et al. (2022), the metamorphic conditions from Ceccato et al. (2024) and the overburden from Ma et al. (2022) (PP-08, PP-09, PP-10, PP-18), which lets a reader place the test volume in its regional setting.
4. The citation and figure structure is largely consistent: all 69 reference entries are cited with matching years, figures are numbered and first cited in order, and every numerical statement in the abstract is consistent with the results (PP-04, PP-46, PP-47, PP-48), so a reader can navigate from claims to data.

## Weaknesses

1. The central scale-effect conclusion is stated as a causal result (exclusion of large fractures), although stress stage, flow geometry and specimen selection change together with scale, the 32 MPa stage lies above every in-situ principal stress, the co-located plug and interval pairs are never compared, what one data point in Fig. 9 is remains undefined, and the laboratory extremes in text and Fig. 8 disagree (GB-03, GB-17, GB-21, GB-41, GB-53). Suggestion: report a plug-by-interval table with ratios against fracture content, use the stress stage nearest to in-situ conditions, define a data point, and state the scale difference as an observation with its candidate causes.
2. The statements on fault zone architecture rest on evidence that does not carry them as written: two crosshole values of similar size cannot localise flow in the core, the BO6 core interval has a lower pore pressure than its neighbours, packer integrity is not reported for the fault core intervals, and the crosshole inputs are not given (GB-28, GB-30, GB-40, GB-56). Suggestion: base the main flow path statement on the single-hole data or report crosshole observations that localise flow, restrict the compartmentalisation statement to BO5, and report integrity and Thiem inputs per test.
3. Reproducibility is limited: the data statement names no dataset, code is available only on request, the second campaign is undated and undocumented, and the laboratory evaluation inputs, the definition of field means and outliers, the porosity conversion and the instruments are not described (GB-19, GB-20, GB-25, GB-27, GB-29, GB-61, GB-62). Suggestion: add a data and code inventory, a campaign table, a per-plug evaluation table, the definition of interval statistics, the porosity formula with densities, and an instrument table.
4. Several moderate inconsistencies between text, figures and sources remain: the Vp-only borehole is misnamed, the vertical stress differs between text and Fig. 2, BO6 facies and interval assignments disagree with Figs. 2 and 7, the P10 comparison between boreholes is neither quantified nor corrected for orientation, four cited works are missing from the reference list, and three citations do not contain what they are cited for (GB-07, GB-08, GB-14, GB-24, GB-37, GB-39, GB-43, GB-51). Suggestion: correct these locations and add an interval table with depths and facies.
