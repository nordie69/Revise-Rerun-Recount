## Summary

The manuscript is an observational case study of one fault zone, the MC fault in the Rotondo granite of the Bedretto Underground Laboratory (about TM2363 to TM2400, overburden stated as 1 to 1.5 km), characterized before the FEAR fluid injection experiments. Two inclined boreholes (BO5, 14 m section shown; BO6, 10 m section shown) were logged with optical and acoustic televiewers and full wave sonic, and tested with a double straddle packer system in eleven 0.73 m intervals (seven in BO5, four in BO6) using pulse, constant rate and shut-in tests evaluated with nSIGHTS, type curves, Cooper-Jacob and Horner methods and the generalized radial flow model; injectivities and two crosshole permeabilities come from a second campaign with 2.4 m and 3.7 m intervals. Eleven plugs from BO5 covering facies F1 to F4 were tested for permeability at isotropic effective stresses of 15, 25 and 32 MPa, and plugs and core segments were measured for WIP and He porosity, P-wave velocity, XRD and CT porosity. Field permeability spans about 10^-17 to nearly 10^-10 m^2 (BO5 interval means 5.6 × 10^-16 to 1.6 × 10^-11 m^2), laboratory permeability 2.0 × 10^-19 to 2.0 × 10^-13 m^2 by the text, and the two crosshole values are 1.6 and 3.9 × 10^-15 m^2. The authors conclude that laboratory tests underestimate in-situ permeability because plugs exclude large fractures, that the fault core is the main flow path, and that the fault is a mix of localized and distributed conduit after Caine et al. (1996) without a continuous low-permeability core.

## Strengths

1. The study measures the same fault zone at plug, core segment, packer interval and crosshole scale with one facies scheme and three effective stress stages for every plug, and the counts are consistent throughout (11 intervals, 7 + 4; 11 plugs, 3 + 2 + 4 + 2; 33 stress stages; PP-14). Eight of the eleven plugs lie inside tested intervals (GB-05), so the design allows the co-located comparison that the manuscript announces, although it is not yet reported (GB-22).
2. The field logs are reported in a way that can be traced: the values stated in Sect. 4.1 for Vp, Vs, porosity, pore pressure and injectivity agree with independent measurements on Figs. 6 and 7 within reading accuracy, and the field bars of Fig. 10 match the box plot extremes of Figs. 6 and 7 within 0.12 decades (PP-28, PP-30, PP-45). A reader can therefore check most field statements against the figures, with the exceptions listed in GB-41 and GB-60.
3. The manuscript states many of its own limitations where they arise: samples from BO5 only, plugs co-located only "if feasible", packers overlapping fractures near the core, the transfer of the stress field from TM2000, dry and unconfined laboratory Vp, possible packer misplacement in interval 3 and facies overlap in BO6 (PP-21, PP-43). This lets the reader see where single values are uncertain; the findings below take these disclosures up and address only consequences that are not drawn.
4. The geological and stress setting rests on sources that support it: the stress magnitudes and orientation match Bröker and Ma (2022), and the facies definitions, lineament sets and fault extent match Achtziger-Zupančič et al. (2024) (PP-12, PP-07); field and laboratory data are deposited with a DOI under CC BY 4.0 (PP-55). The setting can thus be checked against its sources, and the raw data are in principle available, although their content is not described (GB-66).

## Weaknesses

1. The central scale effect is attributed to the exclusion of large fractures from plugs, but stress state, normalisation length and sample position change together with scale, and the comparison in Fig. 9 weights facies by repeated evaluations and leaves out facies 0 (GB-05, GB-52, GB-21, GB-22, GB-15). Suggestion: pair each plug with its interval at the stress stage nearest to in-situ conditions, count one value per plug and stage and per interval, state the facies assignment rule, and report transmissivity alongside interval permeability.
2. The statements on fault architecture are stronger than the evidence: the main flow path through the core rests on two crosshole tests that do not contrast core and off-core paths, the compartmentalization holds in BO5 but not in BO6 as plotted, the decrease of permeability at the largest scale rests on two points placed at a scale they do not have, and the Conclusion turns tentative readings into findings (GB-57, GB-60, GB-55, GB-61, GB-62). Suggestion: restate these as hypotheses, report the flow dimension and pore pressure origin per interval, place the crosshole values at their inter-well distance, and name the crosshole design that would separate the explanations.
3. The contribution announced first in the Abstract, constraints for selecting injection sites, is not delivered as a result, and the practical recommendations of the outlook are not derived from the data or run against the manuscript's own observations (GB-02, GB-63, GB-64, GB-65). Suggestion: add a synthesis table of the eleven intervals with permeability, pore pressure, injectivity and the selection decision, and mark the outlook claims as hypotheses with the measurement that would test them.
4. Several results cannot be reproduced or do not match between text and figures: the evaluation chain and code, the Vp-derived porosity log, the per-interval results, the laboratory permeability values, the BO6 facies statements, the Vs logging statement, the stress magnitudes, the appendix sample values and four missing references (GB-27, GB-31, GB-38, GB-44, GB-41, GB-30, GB-17, GB-47, GB-53). Suggestion: add results tables per interval and per plug, deposit the evaluation code with the data, give all inputs of the porosity conversion, and correct the values against the data set.
